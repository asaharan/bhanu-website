## Notes on licensing

- **Pexels License** and **Pixabay Content License**: both allow free commercial use,
  modification, and redistribution as part of a larger work, with no attribution
  required. Verified on pexels.com/license and pixabay.com/service/license/.
- **Unsplash License**: free for commercial and noncommercial use, no permission or
  attribution required. Verified the specific doctor-portrait photo is NOT an
  "Unsplash+" (Getty-licensed, paid) photo — confirmed free under the standard
  Unsplash License.
- **Wikimedia Commons / CDC PHIL public domain files** (`condition-eczema.webp`,
  `condition-fungal-infection.webp`): U.S. government/CDC works and self-released
  works, explicitly dedicated to the public domain — no attribution required.
- **Wikimedia Commons CC BY-SA 4.0 files** (`condition-vitiligo.webp`,
  `condition-urticaria.webp`): these two DO technically require attribution
  ("share-alike") under their license if used in ways Wikimedia's own reuse
  guidance considers "publication." For a low-risk commercial clinic site,
  consider adding a small credits line (e.g., "vitiligo/urticaria reference
  photos via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0") in the site footer, or swap
  these two for a fully public-domain / attribution-free alternative if strict
  no-attribution is required.

All skin-condition photos were chosen to be clinical/reference-style (dermatology
documentation or stock photography), not graphic or distressing, consistent with
standard imagery used on dermatology/Ayurveda clinic websites to illustrate the
conditions they treat.
